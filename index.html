What We Heard From the Business

Revenue and conversions matter most right now

The current priority is not adding complexity for complexity’s sake. The priority is increasing revenue by improving how inbound opportunities are captured, routed, and followed up on.

There is active lead leakage

Calls are missed, forms can arrive without clean routing or visibility, and follow-up activity is not yet centralized enough for confident supervision.

Repeated need: visibility within ~10 minutes

Manual tracking is creating drag

Current processes still rely too heavily on spreadsheets, manual review, and human memory, which creates stress and inconsistency.

Reporting burden is real

The supervisee reporting workflow appears workable in pieces, but it still depends on repetitive human effort, multiple sheets, and manual signature handling.

CRM Decision: Three Practical Paths

The CRM question should be treated as a workflow-fit decision, not just a software preference decision.

Surface A: Select what already exists

Choose the most practical current platform and configure it properly around the real workflow.

  • Likely best example: Zoho
  • Lower cost, lower friction
  • Best for speed and control

Surface B: Build on top of an existing CRM

Use an existing platform as the compliant base, then add workflow-specific improvements alongside it.

  • Preserves platform familiarity
  • Solves clunky or limited areas
  • Strong hybrid option

Surface C: Design a business-specific system

Map the exact business rules, dashboards, and action logic around how Choya actually operates.

  • Most customized
  • Most ambitious
  • Not recommended as the first move
Phase 1 Position: Start with the workflow and compliance realities first, then select the path that best supports those realities. The most practical near-term direction is a Zoho-centered setup or compliant hybrid workflow.

HIPAA and Platform Considerations

The first phase should not be designed around pushing sensitive workflows into tools that are not clearly aligned with the compliance requirements of the business.

What matters

  • Understanding what data needs to be captured
  • Separating operationally useful data from legally sensitive data
  • Making communication and storage patterns intentional
  • Avoiding assumptions that “popular” equals “safe”

Why this matters for Phase 1

This is one of the strongest arguments for a more measured Zoho-first or compliant-hybrid approach instead of forcing a platform that looks better but may fit the constraints worse.

Recommended 30-Day Action Path

1

Week 1: Clarify and map

Confirm lead sources, missed-call handling, form submission paths, dashboard requirements, and which data must stay inside compliant boundaries.

2

Week 2: Stand up the operational structure

Configure or prototype the initial workflow path, basic dashboard visibility, owner-based follow-up handling, and first response-status structure.

3

Week 3: Validate usage and friction

Observe what the team actually uses, identify clunky points, and measure whether visibility and response speed improved.

4

Week 4: Refine and decide the next move

Decide whether to deepen the platform configuration, add a supporting custom layer, or justify a later broader system build.

Priority 1 Recommendation

Based on the assessment, the most important immediate area for Choya to address is the CRM and revenue workflow layer tied to missed calls, forms, follow-up, and dashboard visibility.

The first decision should not be treated as a permanent all-or-nothing system choice. It should be treated as a practical first move that helps the business reduce leakage, improve visibility, and create more confidence in the path forward.

Priority 1 Conclusion: Choya should first select the CRM and workflow path that best supports fast follow-up, clear dashboard visibility, and HIPAA-conscious operations, while preserving room to build on top of that foundation later if needed.
